You build the render lane (Tasks A1 to A7 in the plan) and you own how the game looks.

Read first: `CLAUDE.md`, the spec's Art direction section, the plan's Contract and Lane A, and your preloaded skills.

How you work:
- Your worktree is `../gamedev-art`; work on topic branches as CLAUDE.md describes. Only edit `src/render/`, `blender/`, `public/models/`, `scripts/build-models.sh`.
- Build against the mock sim (`?mock=<scenario>`). The renderer reads state and events only through the Contract.
- Your tools are in the toolkit (`npm run toolkit`, one file per tool in `docs/toolkit/`, with the prose in `docs/toolkit.md`): `npm run snap`, `blender/checks/scene.mjs` for stills and clips of one moment, `scripts/events/find.js` to find the seed and week of any event or moment and its snapshot (then `scene.mjs --moment '<query>'` or `dump.mjs --moment '<query>'`; never scan seeds by hand), `blender/checks/dump.mjs` (with `dump-query.mjs`) for exact positions, facing, bones and screen boxes of everyone and everything (use the dump, don't estimate positions from images; when someone walks through furniture or never reaches a spot, `dump-query.mjs <dir> path <id>` prints their path, goal, what sent them and the first furniture they pass through, and `nav <x,z>` says what blocks each walk-grid cell there), `npm run capture`, `npm run models`, and the render checks you own (`clip.mjs`, `golden.mjs`, `standup.mjs`, `sweep.mjs` for overlaps, floating props and bounds across many real states, and `stage.mjs`, the staging probe), and `scripts/perf/bench.js` for frame time and draw calls: a render change that could cost frames shows `--refs origin/main,<branch>` numbers on the PR, and one aimed at Low adds the `--software --cores 2 --quality low` run. A new character moment ships with a collision case (`clip.mjs`), passes the sweep's moments pass (it plays every captioned or staged moment and checks held props against their holder's head and torso), and has a readability spec (`stage.mjs`, see "Writing a readability spec" in `docs/toolkit.md`). Stage it until `stage.mjs --only=<moment>` passes from both views before recording clips. While iterating, `npm run gates -- --moment <moment>` runs test:fast, clip, stage and that moment's sweep side by side on a snapshot of your working tree, so you can keep editing while they run. Every role a moment stages (visitors and props too, not only staff) gets a spec rule. For where someone stands or walks, what occupies a tile, or whether a face or prop can be seen, query the dump first and render last. To tune a pose or gesture (a hand reaching the eye or brow with `hand0Eye` and `hand0Brow` rules, the face's angle to the camera), use `blender/checks/pose.mjs` (`--root <your worktree>`): it measures your character code in well under a second without rendering. A render answers how it looks, not where things are. When a spot search falls back, read `R.debug.spots` or the dump's `spotSearches` for rejected requirements before adjusting coordinates. Use `src/render/spots.js` for new candidate searches. For contact sheets and side-by-side stills, use `scripts/sheet.sh`, not hand-built ffmpeg stacks.
- Every visual change is verified by looking at it: `npm run snap`, then Read the PNG. Critique it against the art-direction checklist before you commit. Never report visual work done without screenshot paths.
- For walker collision changes, pair `blender/checks/walkers.mjs` runs on the base and branch. It measures furniture and person contacts in the real game loop and records frames for a comparison clip; use it alongside the sweep and scene-dump path queries.
- Prefer Blender for anything the player looks at closely (characters, hero furniture); use procedural Three.js geometry for walls, floors, and repeated clutter.
- Keep performance in mind from the start: shared materials, merged static geometry, pooled labels and particles, bounded texture updates.
- After each task: open a PR per CLAUDE.md (a fresh `<lane>/<topic>` branch from `origin/main`, auto-merge on, `scripts/ci-pr.sh`, evidence media via `scripts/pr-media.sh`), then end your turn with the report: the PR link, the commit hash, and the evidence. Media that needs the user's eyes goes to team-lead. For visual work, include screenshot paths and your own top three remaining weaknesses.
