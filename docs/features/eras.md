# Eras and time of day

- **Pre-internet founding**: select Pre-internet with `?eras`. Two founders start in a three-desk garage with $240K added to the selected funding, Ops and retail open, no skipped goals and score x0.72, targeting a median score share of 95% of Classic. A compressed 1990 to 1997 chapter lasts 156 company weeks, followed by 208 weeks each of dot-com and Web 2.0, then twenty modern years. Dot-com beats count from entry into that chapter. A company carrying a larger payroll out of physical distribution faces a harder dot-com bust than a two-founder dot-com start. Existing historical saves retain their saved routes and score factors. `id: preinternet` Media: [still](https://github.com/justinlindh/human-in-the-loop/blob/feature-media/era-still-preinternet.webp?raw=true), [clip](https://github.com/justinlindh/human-in-the-loop/blob/feature-media/era-preinternet.mp4?raw=true) ([preview](https://github.com/justinlindh/human-in-the-loop/blob/feature-media/era-preinternet.gif?raw=true)).
- **Physical releases**: choose Boxed Software in Build, then use Reports > Inventory, marked with a box icon. Order 100, 500 or 1,000 copies at $8 each before discounts. Delivery takes two playable weeks. Product appeal and health drive demand; sales consume stock at $250 a copy, less the retailer's 30% share. Reviews below 6 produce one return per twenty eligible copies, carrying the remaining eligible units between weeks. Valuation includes the last 52 weeks of box receipts after retailer fees and refunds, using the same annual revenue multiple as service sales. Installed copies remain separate from subscribers and service MRR through every chapter. Copies age out over the years (one in 520 a week, about a ten-year life), and their owners come back for the current version, so a product that has sold to its whole market sells again. Start a separate On-prem or web product for recurring service contracts. The six Reports tabs wrap on phones; Build keeps the inventory link above each work tab. `id: boxed`
- **Mastering, shelf clearance and CDs**: each pre-internet boxed release offers master verification for $2K and five reliability/maximum-health points, or a deadline bonus of $3K with three debt. Four weeks after its first delivery, the retailer withdraws remaining stock and charges 20% of its manufacturing cost, capped at $8K, once per product. Chapter week 78 offers CD mastering for $4K; the next batch holds 25% more paid copies. A first delivered physical batch earns $5K and two brand. `id: pre_master_disk` `id: pre_retail_returns` `id: pre_cd_rom` `id: pre_first_batch`
- **Patches by post**: maintenance slows boxed-product wear; restoring health requires a mailed patch at $2 per installed copy, capped at $10K. Fresh copies include the launch version. At full health with the current version installed, Mail patch is disabled with the reason "Installed copies are already patched"; dispatch refuses it without charging, posting chat or showing a toast. A version update can be mailed even at full health. Reports shows the price, current and restored health, installed release version, stock, deliveries, sales, customer returns, net box receipts, retailer fees, refunds, manufacturing, buyback and patch costs. The patch posts an original DeskNet line. `id: pre_patch_by_post`
- **Market clock**: in every `?eras` career, the market's difficulty follows the calendar from 2019: rival clones, review expectations, project size, market size and the talent pool. Through the early chapters it stays at the 2019 level, so a company that reaches Classic meets the market a fresh Classic founding meets. A ChatGBT or Agents founding meets the market of its calendar year. Company costs (incident bills, tenure, cooldowns, salaries) follow company age. In a Classic career the two clocks are the same. The market stops getting harder in 2039, where a Classic career's twenty years end, so a late founding and any company that keeps playing past its anniversary meet a steady market. To see it, found a dot-com company and compare new project sizes in 1999 and 2020 with a Classic founding's first year.
- **Exit bars by start**: the MRR an IPO or an acquisition offer needs follows the founding era, so each start retires about as often as Classic: x1.3 for pre-internet, x1.35 for dot-com, x1.2 for Web 2.0, x0.8 for ChatGBT, x0.75 for Agents, x0.65 for Consolidation and x0.45 for the Plateau ($2.85M and $2.55M in Classic). Brand and HQ requirements are unchanged. `id: preinternet` `id: dotcom` `id: web2` `id: chatgbt` `id: agents` `id: consolidation` `id: plateau`
- **The long career**: the Pre-internet founding is the long career: 156 weeks of boxed software, 208 of dot-com, 208 of Web 2.0, then twenty modern years through the Plateau, 1,612 playable weeks in all. Ten- and twenty-year company birthdays along the way do not end it; the career ends at the modern twentieth anniversary. The founders' last big bet waits until about three years before that end in any chaptered career, as it does in Classic. A saved career keeps the chapter lengths it was founded with. The sim names it `long_career` and a Classic founding `classic_career` for the founding screen. `id: preinternet`

- **Period wardrobe**: with `?eras`, a founded historical career dresses staff and visitors for its current era.
